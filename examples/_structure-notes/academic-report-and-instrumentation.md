# Structure notes: academic-skills report and instrumentation report (anonymised)

Source: two anonymised student reports, an academic-skills literature-style report (volcanic eruptions) and an electronics/instrumentation report (Parts A and B).

## CALIBRATION
Mid-range student work (roughly a 2:2); marks and feedback unknown. BASELINE only. Use for work type, cover sheet, section order, module context; not for discussion style or referencing.

## Literature-style report (~2,400 w incl. cover sheet and references)
- Cover sheet, title block (name, "<MODULE> Report", topic, date), Abstract, hand-typed Table of Contents with dotted leaders, then Introduction, Relevance, Methods, "How volcanoes erupt", Literature review, Discussion, Conclusion, References. Footer "Academic Year 2025-26" and page numbers.
- Headings are List Paragraph (not real Heading styles), so the TOC is manual. The cover sheet has a placeholder "Enter the Module code here" left unfilled; "Referencese" typo in a heading.
- Literature review summarises two papers (Chen; Plaza et al.) with in-text naming of authors ("According to Chen...", "Pablo et al. highlight..."), which is the one place where in-prose attribution appears. Discussion compares them but mostly restates; conclusion short (about 70 w).
- References: 12 entries, mixed quality (Britannica, Internet Geography, Reuters, a foundation website, USGS pages, one peer-reviewed article). Defects: `(n.d.)` on sources that have dates, `?utm` tracking parameter, foreign-language phrase left in an entry, missing access dates, misspelt institution names.
- Good to keep: real heading hierarchy idea (Introduction > Relevance > Methods), named-author attribution in the literature review. Bad: padded relevance, claims with figures ("800,000,000 people") not tied to a named numbered source in the text.

## Instrumentation report (Parts A and B, ~3,300 w)
- Part A: technical description report (UV-Vis spectrophotometer): Abstract, manual TOC, Introduction (Aim, Principles and formulas), operation, sources, components, monochromators, data, alternatives, comparison of commercial models (table in an appendix), accuracy and affecting factors, Conclusion, Appendices, References (8 entries including manufacturer datasheets, which is good practice).
- Part B: calculation report: Abstract, TOC, Introduction, Given data and conversion, Output vs applied torque (graph), Error% calculations (FSD error, overall error, systematic, random, hysteresis, each with numbered formula, Excel-made table pasted as an image), "How tables and graphs were made", Conclusion, References (lecture slides only).
- Numbered equations (1) to (11); tables as images with captions "Table N <title>"; graphs "Graph N".
- Numbering inconsistent again (headings numbered "5.", "6." in places only; "Table of condense" typo).
- Good to keep: method-transparency section ("how tables and graphs were made"), manufacturer datasheets as sources, formula per error type.
- Weak: descriptive prose without in-text sources, conclusion restating.

## Cover sheet (all six works)
Typical UK "Coursework Cover Sheet": school, assessment title, course, module title/code, year of study (2025-26), student number, misconduct declaration, checklist (learning outcomes, Turnitin, references per brief, proofread, word count, AI use), AI tools box, well-being/mitigating circumstances, three reflection questions. Fill all fields (one of the examples still had an unfilled placeholder).
